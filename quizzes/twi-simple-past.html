🎯 The simple past tense describes actions that have already happened

⏪ What is the Simple Past Tense?

The simple past tense is used to talk about actions or events that have already happened — anything that took place before the present moment (a second ago, yesterday, last week, last year, etc.).

💡 Formation depends on:
  • Whether the verb is followed by an object or adverb
  • Whether the verb ends in a vowel or a consonant
  • Whether the subject is a pronoun (combined) or noun (separate)
📜 Rule A: Verb + Object/Adverb

Rule: Double the final letter of the verb (whether it ends in a vowel or consonant).

Rule: Add suffix -iɛ or -eɛ (depends on vowel harmony).

📜 Rule C: No Object, Verb ends in CONSONANT

Rule: First add -i or -e (vowel harmony), then add -ɛ → -iɛ or -eɛ.

Original VerbPast FormExample Sentence

📜 Rule D: Serialized Verbs (Verb + Verb pairs)

Examples: to twene (throw away), sɔ hwɛ (test), koto sa (bend down and dance), soa kɔ (carry away)

Rule: First verb stays the same. Apply past tense rule to the second verb only. Pronoun subjects combine with the first verb.

Original PairPast FormExample Sentence
to tweneto tweneeɛKofi to tweneeɛ. (Kofi threw away.)

📜 Rule E: Expressing "Used to" (Habitual Past)

Rule: To express actions that used to happen in the past, keep the verb in its present/infinitive form and begin the sentence with Na.

Na Ama nom nsuo.
Ama used to drink water.
Na me dua aburoo.
I used to plant maize.
