🔢 Countable vs Uncountable Nouns

Countable nouns have both singular and plural forms. They refer to things that can be counted as separate entities.

Uncountable nouns do not have a plural form. They refer to substances, liquids, grains, or abstract ideas that cannot be counted individually.

📝 Usage Examples – Countable Nouns
Akwadaa no se ɔne sukuu nkwadaa no nni agorɔ.
The child says he/she will not play with the school children.
Opolisini no ada.
The police officer has slept.
Ntere no yɛ sɛn?
How many are the spoons?
Kraman no awo; yɛanya nkraman pii.
The dog has given birth; we've gotten many dogs.
Ɛdan baako pɛ na w'ani agye saa no? Yɛwɔ adan nsia.
Just one house and you are this happy? We have six houses.
💧 Uncountable Nouns (Nneɛma a yɛntumi nkan)
📌 Note: Uncountable nouns denote things that cannot be counted. For example, you cannot count water (nsuo).
Abstract / Qualities
  • ahoɔden - strength
  • nimdeɛ - knowledge
  • aboterɛ - patience
  • tumi - power/authority
  • asoɔden - stubbornness
Food / Consumables
  • ɛmo - rice
  • asikyire - sugar
  • nsa(den) - alcohol
  • sika - money
  • kaseɛ - news
Feelings / Emotions
  • ɔdɔ - love
  • anigyeɛ - happiness
  • awerɛhoɔ - sadness
  • afotuo - advice
Other Common Ones
  • anyinam ahoɔden - electricity
  • mframa - air/wind
  • nsuo - water
  • nufusuo - milk
📝 Usage Examples – Uncountable Nouns
Aboterɛ wie nkonimdie.
Patience ends in victory.
Tumi na wode kasa.
You speak with authority.
Kofi wɔ ahoɔden.
Kofi has strength.
Tɔ asikyire ma me.
Buy me sugar.
Wonom nsa dodo.
You drink too much alcohol.
Onyankopɔn nhyira wo ahoɔden.
May God bless you with strength.
