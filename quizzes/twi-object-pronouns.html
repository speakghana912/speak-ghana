🎯 Object pronouns receive the action of the verb (me, you, him, her, us, them)

🎯 What is an Object Pronoun?

An object pronoun is a personal pronoun used as the grammatical object of a verb — either as a direct object or an indirect object.

💡 Examples: In English, object pronouns are: me, you, him, her, it, us, them

📝 Usage Examples (Direct Objects)

📝 Usage Examples (Indirect Objects)
Amakye maa me akonnwa.
Amakye gave me a chair.
Wo papa gyaa wo sika.
Your father left you money.
Metwaa no twɛdeɛ.
I gave him/her a knock.
Akosua twerɛɛ yɛn krataa.
Akosua wrote us a letter.
Mede nwoma no akyɛ mo.
I have gifted you (plural) the book.
Anɔkye wuo no gyaa wɔn awerɛhoɔ.
Anokye's death left them sadness.
📌 Important Notes
1. Form Changes: Most pronouns stay the same whether used as subject or object.
Only ɔno (he/she) and ɛno (it) change when used as objects — they both become no.
2. Direct vs Indirect Objects: Object pronouns can function as either direct objects (directly affected by the verb) or indirect objects (recipient of the direct object).
3. No Combining with Verbs: Unlike subject pronouns, object pronouns are not combined with verbs when writing. They remain separate.
