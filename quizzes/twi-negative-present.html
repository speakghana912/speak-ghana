🚫 What is the Negative Present Tense?

In Twi, to form the negative in the present tense (simple present / habitual), we use the negation marker "n-" or "m-" placed before the verb.

💡 The exact form depends on:
  • The first letter/sound of the verb (vowel harmony and consonant rules)
  • Whether the subject is a pronoun or a full noun
📜 Main Rules for Negative Present
Rule 1: For verbs beginning with consonants (especially m, b, p, f, etc.)
→ Use m- before the verb (it often assimilates or changes based on the verb's initial sound)
Rule 2: For verbs beginning with vowels or other consonants
→ Use n- before the verb
Rule 3: Pronoun subjects often merge with the negation marker

📝 Examples in Sentences (Negative Present)
Mennom nsuo.
I do not drink water. (or: I am not drinking water.)
Wonnkɔ sukuu.
You do not go to school.
Ɔntɔ borɔdeɛ.

Yɛnnni aduane.
We do not eat food.
Monnhu no.
You (pl.) do not see him/her/it.
Wɔnni adowa.
They do not have deer.
Kofi nnom nsuo.
Kofi does not drink water.
Akosua nni fufu.
Akosua does not eat fufu.
📌 Key Notes
✓ The negative marker often causes the verb to take a nasal sound (n/m).

✓ For verbs starting with b, p, f, m, the negative frequently uses m- (e.g., menn-, monn-).

✓ For many other verbs, n- is used (e.g., nnom, nni, nkɔ).

✓ The negative present is commonly used for both simple present (habits) and general statements of not doing something now or usually.
