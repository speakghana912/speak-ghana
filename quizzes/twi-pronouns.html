🔤 What is a Pronoun?

A pronoun is a word used to replace a noun or noun phrase to avoid repetition. Twi pronouns help you speak more naturally and fluently.

💡 Key Point: The same word can mean both "I" and "me" — context tells you which!

✂️ Contracted Subject Forms

When a pronoun is directly followed by a verb, they contract:

Full FormContractedExample
ɔnoɔ-ɔ-didi (he/she eats)
ɛnoɛ-ɛ-yɛ (it is)
yɛnyɛ-yɛ-kɔ (we go)
wɔnwɔ-wɔ-ba (they come)
💡 Note: me, wo, and mo usually stay the same.

🌟 Why Pronouns Matter (Avoid Repetition)
❌ Without Pronouns (Repetitive)
Akosua adi mfeɛ du.
Akosua te Nkran, nanso Akosua kɔ sukuu wɔ Kumase.
Akosua gyina gyinapɛn nsia.
Aduane a Akosua pɛ pa ara ne fufuo.
✅ With Pronouns (Smoother)
Akosua adi mfeɛ du.
Ɔ te Nkran, nanso ɔ kɔ sukuu wɔ Kumase.
Ɔ gyina gyinapɛn nsia.
Aduane a ɔ pɛ pa ara ne fufuo.
📝 Example Sentences
Me ne Kofi bɛkɔ
I and Kofi will go (I will go with Kofi)
Wo yɛ barima!
You are a man!
Ɔno na ɔkaeɛ
It was he/she who said it.
Ɛ bɔɔ me
It hit me.
Yɛn nyinaa wɔ ha
We are all here.
🌍 Cultural Note
No gender distinction! Twi does not distinguish between masculine and feminine.
ɔno is used for both "he" and "she". Context tells you which!
