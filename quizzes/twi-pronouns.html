🔤 What is a Subject Pronoun?

A subject pronoun is a pronoun that acts as the subject of a sentence (i.e., it performs the action of the verb).

When a personal pronoun replaces a noun or noun phrase that is the subject of the sentence, it becomes a subject pronoun.

✂️ Rule 1: Combining Pronoun + Verb

When a subject pronoun is directly followed by a verb, combine the pronoun and the verb (including any tense markers) into one word when writing.

Yɛn + bɛ + ware = Yɛbɛware
We will marry
Me + re + kɔ + dware = Merekɔdware
I am going to bathe
Mo + re + twerɛ + nsɔhwɛ = Moretwerɛ nsɔhwɛ
You (plural) are writing an exam
📝 Rule 2: Contracted Forms

When the following pronouns are directly followed by a verb, they change to these shorter forms:

Full FormContracted Form
ɔno (he/she)ɔ
ɛno (it)ɛ
yɛn (we)yɛ
wɔn (they)wɔ
📌 Note: The pronouns me, wo, and mo usually keep their full form and are simply joined to the verb.

💡 Key Takeaways
✓ Subject pronouns PERFORM the action
