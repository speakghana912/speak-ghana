🔄 What is the Present Continuous Tense?

The Present Continuous (also called Present Progressive) describes actions happening right now or around the current time.

💡 English examples: I am going, She is eating, They are sleeping
📜 How to Form It
Rule: Use the auxiliary verb re- (or ri- in some dialects) prefixed to the main verb.
Pronoun subjects merge with re-.
📋 Present Continuous – Pronoun Table

They Wɔ Wɔre- They are going
📝 Examples in Sentences
Merekɔ sukuu.
I am going to school (right now).
Wore nom nsuo.
You are drinking water.
Ɔre di aduan.
He/She is eating food.
Yɛre hwɛ TV.
We are watching TV.
More sa.
You (pl.) are dancing.
Wɔre kɔ fie.
They are going home.
Kofi re noa fufu.
Kofi is cooking fufu.
Akosua re tɔ ntoma.
Akosua is buying cloth.
📝 More Example Sentences
Merebɔ agorɔ.
I am playing.
Ɔrekɔ sukuu nnɛ.
He is going to school today.
Woretwa adwuma.
You are working.
Yɛredi fufuo.
We are eating fufu.
Wɔresi dan foforɔ.
They are building a new house.
📌 Key Notes
✓ re- indicates the action is in progress now.

✓ It is one of the most commonly used tenses in everyday spoken Twi.

✓ For some verbs, ri- may appear (especially in certain dialects or with certain vowel harmony), but re- is the standard in Asante Twi.

✓ You can add time adverbs like nnɛ (today/now), saa bere yi (at this time), etc., for emphasis.
