⚡ Twi Verbs Adeyɔ

🎯 Verbs express actions, existence, or occurrence of a state (eat, go, sleep, learn)

⚡ What is a Verb (Adeyɔ)?

A verb is the most critical part of a Twi sentence, as it expresses the action, existence, or occurrence of a state.

💡 Examples: didi (eat), kɔ (go), da (sleep), sua (learn)
📚 Common Twi Verbs
Basic Actions
  • didi - to eat
  • nom - to drink
  • da - to sleep
  • kɔ - to go
  • ba - to come
Learning & Communication
  • sua - to learn/study
  • kyerɛw - to write
  • kyerɛ - to teach/show
  • ka - to speak/say
  • tie - to listen
Commerce & Actions
  • tɔ - to buy
  • tɔn - to sell
  • hu - to see
  • yɛ - to do/make/be
📝 Verb Structure in a Sentence

In Twi, the basic sentence structure is Subject + Verb + Object (SVO), similar to English.

Me (Subject) + didi (Verb) = Medidi.
I eat.
Kofi (Subject) + tɔ (Verb) + bayerɛ (Object) = Kofi tɔ bayerɛ.
Kofi buys yam.
🔄 Stative vs Active Verbs
Active Verbs

Describe physical or mental actions.

  • kasa - speak
  • tu mmirika - run
  • sa - dance
Stative Verbs

Describe a state or condition. Often don't take the progressive marker "re-".

  • nim - know
  • pɛ - like/want
  • wɔ - have/be at

Essential Verb: "To Be"

In Twi, there isn't just one word for "to be." The word changes based on what follows it:

TwiUsageExample
yɛUsed with nouns/identitiesMeyɛ ɔkyerɛkyerɛni (I am a teacher)
wɔUsed with locationsMewɔ Kumase (I am in Kumasi)
teUsed with states of beingMete ase (I am alive/well)
🎯 Action Verbs: Transitive vs Intransitive
Transitive Verbs

Have direct objects receiving the action.

Kofi soma Ama.
Kofi sends Ama.
Yaa frɛɛ Kwasi.
Yaa called Kwasi.
Intransitive Verbs

Do not need direct objects; can end a sentence.

Yaw sereeɛ.
Yaw laughed.
Wɔaduru.
They have arrived.
