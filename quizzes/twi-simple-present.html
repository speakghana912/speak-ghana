📅 Twi Simple Present Tense Daa Kabea

🎯 The simple present tense describes actions happening now or that happen regularly

📅 What is the Simple Present Tense?

The simple present tense is used to refer to actions, conditions, or experiences that are currently happening or that are always the case. It describes actions that happen regularly (habitually).

💡 Examples in English: I eat, She works, They play

🎉 GOOD NEWS! 🎉

In Twi, there is NO subject-verb agreement! We use the original (root) form of the verb for ALL subjects.

No matter the pronoun, the verb stays the same! Unlike English where we add "-s" for he/she/it.

📖 Twi Simple Present Tense Rules

We only make use of the original, infinitive form of the verb to show the present tense. Whatever Twi verb you know, just use it as it is — nothing changes.

📌 Important Rule:
  • When the subject is a pronoun (me, wo, ɔ, yɛ, mo, wɔ), we combine it and the verb into one word in writing.
  • For subject nouns (Kofi, Ama, etc.), we separate them from the verb.
📝 Examples with Pronouns (Combined Form)
Menom nsuo.
I drink water.
Medi aduane.
I eat food.
Mesoa.
I carry.
Ɔkye nkosua.
He/She fries eggs.
Yɛsom Nyame.
We worship God.
📝 Examples with Nouns (Separated Form)
Ama tɔn nneɛma.
Ama sells things.
Kofi pɛ akenkan.
Kofi likes reading.
Asumadu kyini.
Asumadu roams.
Opoku kasa dodo.
Opoku talks a lot.
Wosua Twi.
You learn Twi.
