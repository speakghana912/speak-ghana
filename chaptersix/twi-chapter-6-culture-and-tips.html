Cultural Notes — Amammerɛ

🚌 Tro-tro Culture

  • The mate's call is rhythmic and fast — listen for "Osu! Osu!" or "Circle! Lapaz!" They repeat it constantly.
  • When paying, pass your money forward. Other passengers will pass it along. Say Medaase when change comes back.
  • Tro-tros are often very crowded. Mia kakra! (Push a little!) is completely normal — do not be offended.
  • To get off, say Me ba ha! loudly and clearly. The mate will tap the roof to signal the driver to stop.
  • Foreigners may be charged more. Know the standard fare before boarding and negotiate if needed.

🚕 Taxi / Dropping Etiquette

  • Always agree on the price before getting in. Say Ɛyɛ sɛn? and bargain if needed.
  • Dropping = shared taxi — cheaper, you share with other passengers going the same direction.
  • Taxi = private hire — more expensive, the car is yours alone.
  • If you know the route, say Fa ha so (Take this way) to avoid being taken on a longer route.
  • Pay when you arrive. Always use your right hand.

🏍️ Okada Safety

  • Always agree on the fare before getting on.
  • Wear a helmet if one is provided. If not, weigh your safety carefully.
  • Avoid okada after dark — use Uber or Bolt instead.
  • Okada is fastest for short distances in congested areas where cars cannot move.
  • Okadas are not officially licensed in some parts of Accra — be aware of local regulations.

📱 Uber / Bolt in Nkran

  • The app is in English, but drivers may greet you in Twi. Respond with Maaha! or Me ho yɛ!
  • Even with the app, confirm your destination: Mekɔ [beaeɛ].
  • Drivers often comment on traffic: "Traffic yɛ den nnɛ!" (Traffic is bad today!)
  • Tip: use Uber/Bolt late at night when tro-tros are no longer running.
  • Prices surge during peak hours and heavy rain — be prepared.

🗺️ Landmark-Based Navigation

  • Most areas in Accra do not use street addresses. Directions use landmarks everyone knows.
  • Common landmark types: asɔre kɛse (big church), petrol gyappe (filling station), traffic light, gua (market), bank.
  • Learn key landmarks near your destination before you travel: "Wonim asɔre kɛse no?"
  • If directions seem unclear, ask 2–3 people to confirm. Accra residents are generally happy to help.
  • Bisa obi (Ask someone) — this is real and practical advice, not just a phrase.

🤝 Asking for Help

  • Asking strangers for directions in Ghana is completely normal and welcomed. Never feel embarrassed.
  • Always greet first: Maakye! or Maaha! — then ask. Skipping the greeting is rude.
  • If you don't understand, say Kasa bɔkɔɔ (Speak slowly). People will gladly repeat.
  • After receiving help, always say Medaase paa! — the effort is appreciated.
  • The phrase Woadi me ɔkye (You have saved me) is a warm and genuine thank-you for real help.

Common Mistakes to Avoid — Mfomso

❌ Mistake: Forgetting to greet before asking for directions. ✅ Fix: Always start with Maakye! or Maaha! before asking Ɛhe na... wɔ? Skipping the greeting is considered rude and you may get a cold response.
❌ Mistake: Confusing nifa (right) and benkum (left). ✅ Fix: Memory trick — nifa sounds like "nee-fah" — your right hand is the one most people eat with. Benkum starts with "ben" — think of it as the other side.
❌ Mistake: Saying Mepɛ kɔ instead of Mekɔ. ✅ Fix: Mekɔ = I'm going (correct and natural). Mepɛ kɔ = I want to go (grammatically awkward). Just say Mekɔ Osu.
❌ Mistake: Using double-n in negatives — mennkɔ / mennba. ✅ Fix: Single n- prefix only: menkɔ, menba, mendu, mensian.
❌ Mistake: Not saying Me ba ha! loud enough and missing your stop. ✅ Fix: Say it clearly and loudly — the tro-tro is noisy. If needed, wave your hand too. The mate will tap the roof to signal the driver.
❌ Mistake: Accepting the first taxi price without bargaining. ✅ Fix: Always bargain: Ɛyɛ dɔ! Yɛ no... The first price is almost never the final price. Knowing a fair fare range before you travel helps.
❌ Mistake: Putting adjectives before nouns (English order). ✅ Fix: In Twi, adjectives come AFTER the noun: ɔkwan kɛse (road big = big road), NOT kɛse ɔkwan.
❌ Mistake: Confusing Kasa bɔkɔɔ with Kɔ bɔkɔɔ. ✅ Fix: Kasa bɔkɔɔ = Speak slowly. Kɔ bɔkɔɔ = Go slowly. Different verbs — kasa (speak) vs kɔ (go).
❌ Mistake: Giving money with the left hand. ✅ Fix: Always use your right hand to give and receive money, food, gifts, and greetings. If you must use both hands, support your right wrist with your left as a sign of respect.
C

Tips for Success — Akwankyerɛ

🗣️

Learn your destination in Twi before you leave

Before any journey, practice saying your destination clearly: Mekɔ Osu / Lapaz / Circle. Tro-tro mates and taxi drivers respond faster when you say the place name confidently.

💰

Know the fare range before you board

Ask a local or a trusted source what the standard tro-tro or taxi fare is for your journey. This lets you negotiate from a position of knowledge: Menim sika no. (I know the price.)

🗺️

Use landmarks in your directions

When asking or giving directions, always mention a landmark. Instead of "on the second street," say Ɛbɛn asɔre kɛse no (Near the big church). This is how Ghanaians navigate, and it works.

🔁

Don't be afraid to ask for repetition

Kasa bɔkɔɔ and Ka no bio are your best friends when getting directions. Using them shows effort, not weakness — Ghanaians appreciate it when you try.

🚌

Ride the tro-tro once just to experience it

Even if you prefer taxis, take a tro-tro at least once — pay, pass money forward, say Me ba ha! when you arrive. It is the most authentic Accra experience and you will use every phrase from this chapter.

😊

Smile and greet — always

A smile and Maakye! before any question will get you much further than asking directly. Ghanaians are warm and helpful — meet that warmth halfway and the city opens up.
