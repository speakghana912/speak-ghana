Kenkan · Reading Passage

Dabi anɔpa, Kofi kɔɔ efie.
Ɔhyiaa ne yɔnko Ama.
Kofi kaa sɛ:
“Maakye Ama! Wo ho te sɛn?”
Ama buae sɛ:
“Maakye Kofi! Me ho yɛ. Mepa wo kyɛw, wo nso ɛ? Me din de Ama. Na wo din de sɛn?”
Kofi kaa sɛ:
“Me din de Kofi. Ɛyɛ me anigyeɛ sɛ mehyiaa wo. Meda wo ase.”
Ama kaa sɛ:
“Meda wo nso ase. Yɛbɛhyia bio!”
Wɔn nyinaa serewee na wɔkɔɔ wɔn kwan.

Nsɛm Nhwehwɛmu (Comprehension Questions)

  • 1. Dabi anɔpa, Kofi kɔɔ ɛhe? (Where did Kofi go?)
  • 2. Kofi hyiaa obi? (Did Kofi meet someone?)
  • 3. Kofi din de sɛn? (What is Kofi's name?)
  • 4. Ama buae sɛ “Me ho yɛ”. Ɛkyerɛ sɛn? (What does “Me ho yɛ” mean?)
  • 5. Kofi kaa “Meda wo ase”. Ɛkyerɛ sɛn? (What does “Meda wo ase” mean?)
  • 6. Wɔn nyinaa yɛɛ dɛn awiei no? (What did they all do at the end?)
Check answers (click)
1. Kofi kɔɔ efie (went home).
2. Ɔhyiaa ne yɔnko Ama (He met his friend Ama).
3. Ne din de Kofi (His name is Kofi).
4. “Me ho yɛ” means “I am fine”.
5. “Meda wo ase” means “Thank you”.
6. Wɔn nyinaa serewii na wɔkɔɔ wɔn kwan (They all smiled and went their way).

Nkyea ne Mmuaeɛ · Greetings & Responses

Greetings honor the person and show obuo (respect). In Ghana, always greet elders first and reply warmly.

Respectful responses to elders:
→ To elder man: Yaa agya (Thank you, father)
→ To elder woman: Yaa ɛna (Thank you, mother)
→ To peer/sibling: Yaa nua
→ General group reply: Yaanom!

Example: Greeting an elder
Mema wo akye, agya! Wo ho te sɛn?
Yaa, me ba! Nyame adom, me ho yɛ. Na wo nsoɛ?
Bɔkɔɔ, medaase.

Grammar Corner · What is a noun?

A noun is a word that is used to identify:

  • a person (e.g. Kofi, Adwoa, Amakye, Ɔsɛe)
  • a place (e.g. Koforidua, Kumase, Nkran, Atonsu)
  • a thing (e.g. akonnwa (chair), towuro (towel), afe (comb), nwoma (book))
  • an animal (e.g. ɔkraman (dog), agyinammoa (cat), abirekyie (goat), pɔnkɔ (horse))
  • an idea, quality, or state (e.g. nokorɛ (truth), anigyeɛ (happiness), awerɛhoɔ (sadness))

Examples of Twi Nouns

Person: Yaw, buroni, bibini, Opoku, Konadu, Asumadu, Ama, Akosua, Kwabena, Kwadwo, ɔsɔfo, ɔkyerɛkyerɛni, ɔsraani, ɔtomfoɔ, Yaa, Boafo
Place: Amɛreka, aburokyire, Abibirem, ayaresabea, asieeɛ, Sunyani, Berekum, Tafo, Kɔnɔngo, Bepɔso, mmarahyɛ badwam
Thing: krataa, ahoma, toa, didipono, ntoma, mpaboa, kasafidie, dadeɛ, kyiniiɛ
Animal: akokɔ, tɛfrɛ, odwan, kɔtɔ, dɛnkyɛm, ntontom, afunumu pɔnkɔ, ɔwɔ
Idea/quality/state: ahummɔborɔ, ahoɔfɛ, animuonyam, abufuo

Nouns are the foundation of every sentence. In Twi, nouns don't change form for gender, but they can be pluralized by adding prefix "nn-" or changing tone.

Nkɔmmɔdie ne Nsɛmmisa · Dialogues & Practice

Role-play — practice aloud with a friend or record yourself.

Dialogue 1: Morning Meeting (Peers)
A: Maakye! Wo ho te sɛn?
B: Yaa nua! Nyame adom, me ho yɛ. Na wo nsoɛ?
A: Nyame adom, ɛyɛ. Wo din de sɛn?
B: Me din de Kofi. Na wo nsoɛ?
A: Me din de Ama. Ɛyɛ me anigyeɛ sɛ mehyia wo!
B: Yɛbɛhyia bio.

Dialogue 2: Greeting an Elder (Worekyea panin)
A: Mema wo akye, agya! Wo ho te sɛn?
B: Yaa me ba! Nyame adom, me ho yɛ.
A: Medaase, agya. Yɛbɛhyia bio.
B: Yoo, nante yie.

Dialogue 3: At the market (Wɔ edwam)
A: Maaha! Wo ho te sɛn?
B: Yaa! Me ho yɛ. Wopɛ dɛn?
A: Mepɛ mako ne gyeene. Ɛsɛn?
B: Cedi aduonnu. Medaase.
A: Yoo, medaase.

Practice Exercises (Build your fluency)

  1. Matching: Connect the greeting to the correct time: (1) Mema wo akye – (Morning) , Mema wo adwo – (Evening), Mema wo aha – (Afternoon).
  2. Fill the blank: A: Maakye! Wo ho te sɛn? B: ________ (answer “I’m fine”).
  3. Introduce yourself: Write a short self-introduction in Twi: your name, where you’re from, and say “nice to meet you”.
  4. Syllable drill: Break these into syllables: Akwaaba, Mema wo akye, Medaase, Yɛbɛhyia bio.
  5. Translation: “Good morning, how are you?” / “My name is… Thank you very much!”
  6. Noun identification: Find 3 nouns in the passage (e.g. Kofi, Ama, efie) and tell if they are person/place/thing.
