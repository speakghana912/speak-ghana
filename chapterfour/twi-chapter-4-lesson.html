Berɛ, Nnafua, Abusua ne Nnɔmma

Chapter 4 · Time, Days of the Week, Family & Numbers

Kɔ so sua!

In Chapters 2 and 3 you learned to greet people, talk about yourself, and count to 20. Now people will ask you deeper questions about your life:

  • Woadi mfeɛ sɛn? (How old are you?)
  • Abɔ sɛn / Ɛyɛ berɛ bɛn? (What time is it?)
  • Ɛnnɛ yɛ da bɛn? (What day is today?)
  • Wo papa te he? (Where does your father live?)
  • Adɛn nti na wopɛ Twi? (Why do you like Twi?)

This chapter gives you the tools to answer and ask these questions confidently, with numbers up to 100, telling time, the days of the week and their cultural meanings, deeper family vocabulary, adjectives to describe people and things, and question patterns for real conversation.

What You Will Achieve

  • Mewɔ mfeɛ aduonu nwɔtwe. (I am 28 years old.)
  • Abɔ nnɔnnum apa ho simma dunnum. (It is quarter past five.)
  • Ɛnnɛ yɛ Yawoada. (Today is Thursday.)
  • Me papa ne me maame te Kumasi. (My father and mother live in Kumasi.)
  • Hwan ni? Ɔyɛ me nua barima. (Who is this? He is my brother.)
  • Adɛn nti na wopɛ Twi? (Why do you like Twi?)

Kenkan · Ɔpɛnpenha Kofi (Kofi's Birthday)

Read first — the rest of the chapter explains how every part works

Read this passage carefully. Notice the numbers, the day, the time, and the family words — the rest of this chapter will teach you exactly how each one works.

Ɔpɛnpenha Kofi — Kofi's birthday with all his family

Ɛnnɛ yɛ Efiada. Today is Friday. Kofi wɔ ne ɔpɛnpenha nnɛ. Ɔwɔ mfeɛ aduonu nwɔtwe nnɛ. Kofi has his birthday today. He is 28 years old today. Ɔsɔre anɔpa nnɔnsia. Ɔdi ne anɔpa aduane, na ɔhyɛ ne ntadeɛ fɛfɛ. He wakes up at six in the morning. He eats his breakfast and puts on his nice clothes. Abɔ nnɔnwɔtwe, ne maame frɛ no. At eight o'clock, his mother calls him. Ɔkaa sɛ: "Kofi, ɔpɛnpenha pa! Wo abɔ mfeɛ aduonu nwɔtwe nnɛ!" She said: "Kofi, happy birthday! You are 28 years old today!" Kofi buae sɛ: "Medaase, maame! Wobɛba anadwonhwere bɛn?" Kofi replied: "Thank you, mother! What time will you come?" Ne maame kaa sɛ: "Mɛba anwummerɛ. Abɔ nnɔnsia." His mother said: "I will come in the evening. At six o'clock." Awia no, Kofi kɔɔ ɛdwam. Ɔtɔɔ aduane ne nneɛma foforo. In the afternoon, Kofi went to the market. He bought food and other things. Wɔ ɛdwam hɔ, ɔhyiaa ne wɔfa ɔpanyin. At the market, he met his elderly uncle. Ne wɔfa bisaa no sɛ: "Woadi mfeɛ sɛn nnɛ, Kofi?" His uncle asked him: "How old are you today, Kofi?" Kofi buae sɛ: "Mewɔ mfeɛ aduonu nwɔtwe." Kofi replied: "I am 28 years old." Ne wɔfa sɛree na ɔkaa sɛ: "Wo na wo yɛ aberante kɛseɛ! Yɛbɛhyia anwummerɛ wɔ wo fie!" His uncle laughed and said: "You are such a big young man! We will meet at your house in the evening!" Anwummerɛ no, ne abusua nyinaa baa. Ne papa, ne maame, ne nuanom, ne nananom — wɔn nyinaa baa. In the evening, all his family came. His father, his mother, his siblings, his grandparents — they all came. Wɔdi, wɔnom, na wɔserew bɔkɔɔ. They ate, drank, and laughed together. Kofi kaa sɛ: "Medaase, me nuanom nyinaa. Ɛyɛ me anigyeɛ pa ara." Kofi said: "Thank you, all my family. I am very happy."

Nsɛmbisa — Comprehension Questions

  • 1. Ɛnnɛ yɛ da bɛn? (What day is today?)
  • 2. Kofi wɔ mfeɛ sɛn? (How old is Kofi?)
  • 3. Ne maame frɛɛ no berɛ bɛn? (What time did his mother call him?)
  • 4. Kofi kɔɔ he awia no? (Where did Kofi go in the afternoon?)
  • 5. Hwan na Kofi hyiaa wɔ ɛdwam? (Who did Kofi meet at the market?)
  • 6. Ne wɔfa bɛba berɛ bɛn? (When did his uncle say he would come?)
  • 7. Hwan na baa anwummerɛ no? (Who came in the evening?)

Time Questions and Answers

QuestionAnswer

🌍 Ghanaian Time: Social events often start later than announced. "Mereba" (I am coming) can mean "I am on my way" or "I will come eventually." For business and appointments, however, be on time!

Adjectives — Describing People and Things

The key rule: adjective comes AFTER the noun in Twi

The Golden Rule of Twi Adjectives

In English you say "the big man." In Twi, the adjective comes after the noun:

NOUN + ADJECTIVE

  • onipa kɛseɛ — the big/tall man (man big)
  • ba ketewa — the small child (child small)
  • ntadeɛ fɛfɛ — beautiful clothes (clothes beautiful)
  • aduane dɛ — delicious food (food sweet/delicious)

The Three Question Patterns

Pattern 1 — Question word at the END (most common)

Wote he?
Wodi dɛn?
Wobɛba berɛ bɛn?
Wowɔ mma ahe?

Pattern 2 — Question word + "na" + statement

Hwan na ɔreba? (Who is coming?)
Dɛn na wopɛ? (What do you want?)
He na worekɔ? (Where are you going?)
Adɛn nti na woresua Twi? (Why are you learning Twi?)

Pattern 3 — Polite openers

Mepa wo kyɛw, wo din de sɛn?
Mesrɛ wo, wobɛboa me?
Ɛyɛ sɛ wote Twi?

Grammar Deep Dive

Numbers come AFTER nouns

  • nnipa baako — one person (person one)
  • mma mmienu — two children (children two)
  • sika ɛnum — five cedis (money five)

The Verb "wɔ" — to have / to be at (with numbers)

  • Mewɔ mfeɛ aduonu. (I am 20 years old.)
  • Ɔwɔ sika. (He has money.)
  • Yɛwɔ nuanom baasa. (We have three siblings.)

Negative — nni:

  • Me nni sika. (I do not have money.)
  • Ɔ nni fie. (He is not at home.)

The Verb "yɛ" — extremely versatile

  • Ɔyɛ ɔkyerɛkyerɛni. (He is a teacher.) — to be
  • Dɛn na woyɛ? (What do you do?) — to do
  • Ɛyɛ dɛ. (It is sweet.) — quality
  • Ɛyɛ berɛ mmiɛnsa. (It is 3 o'clock.) — time

"Ahe?" vs "Sɛn?" — How many vs How much

  • Ahe? — for countable things: Mma ahe? (How many children?)
  • Sɛn? — for uncountable or general: Sika sɛn? (How much money?)
  • Note: sometimes they overlap — context is key.
