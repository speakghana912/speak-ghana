Cultural Notes for Daily Life

🏠 In the Home — Wɔ Fie Mu

  • Sharing food: When you eat with others, it's common to say "Yɛn di!" (Let's eat!) before starting.
  • Greeting elders: Always greet elders when you enter a room or come home. "Mema wo akye, nana!"
  • Chores are shared: Everyone helps with sweeping (pra), cooking (noa), and washing (hohoro).

🛒 At the Market — Wɔ edwam

  • Greet before you buy: Always say "Maakye!" or "Maaha!" before asking prices.
  • Bargaining is normal: "Ne boɔ yɛ den! Te boɔ no so kakra!" (It's expensive! Reduce the price a little!)
  • Know your numbers: Practice numbers 1–20 before you go.

💼 At Work — Wɔ Adwumayɛbea

  • Morning greetings: Always greet colleagues when you arrive: "Mema wo akye, me yɔnko!"
  • Asking for help: "Mepa wo kyɛw, boa me." (Please, help me.)
  • Thanking: "Medaase" — always say it, always mean it.

🤝 In the Community

  • Children greet adults first: If you're younger, always greet older people before they greet you.
  • Respect for elders: Use "agya" (sir) for older men and "ɛna" (ma) for older women.
  • Handshake: A light handshake with the right hand is standard. For elders, you may support your right hand with your left.
B

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Mistake: Forgetting to put the pronoun before the verb. ✅ Fix: Always say "Medi" not just "di" when talking about yourself.
❌ Mistake: Using the wrong tone for "kɔ" (go) vs "ko" (fight). ✅ Fix: Remember — "kɔ" with open 'ɔ' means go; "ko" with 'o' means fight.
❌ Mistake: Saying "Me nni fufu" when you mean "I don't eat fufu" vs "I don't have fufu." ✅ Fix: "Me nni fufu" = I don't have fufu. "Menni fufu" = I don't eat fufu. (Note the double 'n')
❌ Mistake: Forgetting that "te" can mean live, hear, or sit. ✅ Fix: Let context guide you. "Mete Accra" = I live in Accra. "Mete aseɛ" = I understand.
❌ Mistake: Using "daa" (every day) vs "da" (sleep/day). ✅ Fix: "Daa" has a long 'a' sound; "da" is short.
C

Tips for Success in Ghana

🛒

Practice with market sellers

Go to a Ghanaian market (or African market near you) and try buying something in Twi. Start with "Mepa wo kyɛw..."

🗣️

Talk to yourself

Describe your day in Twi while you're doing it. "Mɛkɔ adwuma." "Mɛdi fufu." "Mɛda anadwo."

📺

Watch Ghanaian vlogs

YouTube has many Ghanaian vloggers. Listen for the verbs you learned.

🎵

Sing along

Ghanaian music (highlife, afrobeats) is full of everyday phrases. Try to catch words like "dɔ," "pɛ," "ba," "kɔ."

💬

Text in Twi

Send WhatsApp messages to your classmates or teacher in Twi. Start simple: "Wo ho te sɛn nnɛ?"

📅

Daily challenge

Every day, learn one new word and use it in a sentence. Write it down!
