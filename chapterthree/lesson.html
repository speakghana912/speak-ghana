Me ne Me Daadaa Mu Nsɛm

Chapter 3 · Talking About Yourself and Your Daily Life

Akwaaba bio!

In Chapter 2 you learned how to greet people and introduce yourself. Now Ghanaians will ask you real questions about your life:

  • Wodi deɛn? (What do you eat?)
  • Wote he? (Where do you live?)
  • Woyɛ deɛn? (What do you do?)
  • Wo ho te sɛn nnɛ? (How are you today?)
  • Wopɛ deɛn? (What do you like?)

This chapter teaches you to answer those questions in Twi with confidence. You will use the short words (me, wo, di, kɔ, ba, te, pɛ…) you already met in Chapters 1 and 2 — now you will build real sentences about your life, your day, your food, your home, and your family.

What You'll Achieve in This Chapter

By the end of this chapter you will be able to say things like:

  • Mete Accra. (I live in Accra.)
  • Medi banku daa. (I eat banku every day.)
  • Mepɛ fufu pa ara. (I really like fufu.)
  • Mekɔ sukuu / adwuma daa. (I go to school/work every day.)
  • Me ne me nua na ɛte. (I live with my sibling.)
  • Me papa yɛ ɔkyerɛkyerɛni. (My father is a teacher.)

Let's begin — with a story you can read right now.

A

Kenkan · Me Daadaa Abrabɔ (My Everyday Life)

Read this short passage about a typical day. Don't worry if you don't understand every word yet — the rest of this chapter will teach you exactly how each part works.

Anɔpa biara, mesɔre nnɔnsia. Medware, mehyɛ me ntadeɛ, na medi anɔpa aduane. Mepɛ ɛmo ne nkosua pa ara. Mekɔ adwuma anɔpa nnɔnwɔtwe, ɛfiri sɛ mete Kumasi nanso meyɛ adwuma wɔ Ejisu — ɛkwan no ware, ɛno nti ɛsɛ sɛ mesɔre ntɛm.

Awia, medi banku ne nsuomunam. Anwummerɛ, meba fie na menoa aduane. Me ne me nua baa na ɛte.

Anadwo, yɛhwɛ kasamfonin — In the evening, we watch TV together.

Anadwo, yɛhwɛ kasamfonin. Ɔbisa me sɛ: "Wopɛ Twi asua no?" Mebua sɛ: "Aane, mepɛ no paa!" Yɛsere bɔkɔɔ, na afei yada.

Nsɛmbisa (Comprehension Questions)

  • 1. Berɛ bɛn na ɔsɔre anɔpa? (What time does she wake up?)
  • 2. Dɛn na ɔpɛ sɛ odi anɔpa? (What does she like to eat in the morning?)
  • 3. Ɔte he, na ɔyɛ adwuma he? (Where does she live, and where does she work?)
  • 4. Adɛn nti na ɛsɛ sɛ ɔsɔre ntɛm? (Why does she need to wake up early?)
  • 5. Dɛn na ne nua baa bisa no anadwo? (What does her sister ask her at night?)
Check answers (click)
1. Ɔsɔre nnɔnsia (She wakes up at six).
2. Ɔpɛ ɛmo ne nkosua (She likes rice and eggs).
3. Ɔte Kumasi, nanso ɔyɛ adwuma wɔ Ejisu (She lives in Kumasi, but works in Ejisu).
4. Ɛfiri sɛ ɛkwan no ware (Because the journey/road is long).
5. Ɔbisa no sɛ ɔpɛ Twi asua no (She asks if she likes learning Twi).
💡 Teacher's Note: Look for keywords like sɔre (wake up), te (live), and nua baa (sister) to find your answers. For a challenge, try rewriting the first paragraph using ɔno (he/she) instead of me.

Building Simple Sentences About Your Life

📘 Word Order in Twi Sentences

Twi follows a simple word order:

SUBJECT + VERB + OBJECT + PLACE/TIME

  • Me (subject) + di (verb) + fufu (object) = Medi fufu.
  • Wo (subject) + kɔ (verb) + sukuu (object) + anɔpa (time) = Wokɔ sukuu anɔpa.

Pattern 1: Pronoun + Verb (Just the action)

TwiMeaning
Medi.I eat.
Wokɔ.You go.
Ɔba.He/She comes.
Yɛda.We sleep.
Wɔsa.They dance.

Pattern 2: Pronoun + Verb + What (Object)

TwiMeaning
Me di fufu.I eat fufu.
Mepɛ banku.I like banku.
Metɔ aduane.I buy food.
Ɔka Twi.He/She speaks Twi.
Yɛnom nsu.We drink water.

Pattern 3: Pronoun + Verb + Place / Time

TwiMeaning
Mete Accra.I live in Accra.
Mekɔ sukuu da biara.I go to school every day.
Meda anadwo.I sleep at night.
Ɔkɔ adwuma anɔpa.He goes to work in the morning.
Yɛba fie anwummerɛ.We come home in the evening.

Pattern 4: Simple Questions

TwiMeaning
Wodi dɛn?What do you eat?
Wote he?Where do you live?
Wopɛ dɛn?What do you like?
Woyɛ dɛn?What do you do?
Ɔkɔ he?Where is he/she going?
E

Pattern 5: Saying "No" or "Not" (Negation)

To make a sentence negative, add n- before the verb:

PositiveNegative
Me di fufu. (I eat fufu.)Menni fufu. (I don't eat fufu.)
Mekɔ sukuu. (I go to school.)Menkɔ sukuu. (I don't go to school.)
Mete Accra. (I live in Accra.)Mente Accra. (I don't live in Accra.)
Mepɛ banku. (I like banku.)Mempɛ banku. (I don't like banku.)
⚠️ Important: The prefix n- changes the tone and flow of the word. Practice these pairs:
me di → menni
me kɔ → menkɔ
me te → mente
me pɛ → mempɛ

Grammar Deep Dive: te, wɔ, nim

The Versatile Verb "te"

The word te has several important meanings depending on context:

  • To live / reside: Mete Accra. (I live in Accra.)
  • To hear / understand: Mete aseɛ. (I understand.) / Mente aseɛ. (I don't understand.)
  • To sit / be located: Ɔte fie. (He is at home / sitting at home.)
  • To be in a state: Wo ho te sɛn? (How are you? — lit. How is your body?)

The Verb "wɔ" — To Have / To Be At

Use wɔ to show possession or location:

  • Mewɔ sika. (I have money.)
  • Ɔwɔ fie. (He is at home.)
  • Fufu wɔ hɔ? (Is there fufu?)
  • Menni sika. (I don't have money — negative)

The Verb "nim" — To Know

  • Mennim. (I don't know.)
  • Wonim no? (Do you know him/her?)
  • Mennim sɛ ɔbɛba a. (I don't know if he'll come.)
