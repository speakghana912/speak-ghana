Dwumadi — Shopping, Prices & Bargaining

Chapter 5 · Tɔ, Tɔn, ne Tua Sika — Buy, Sell, and Pay

Yɛkɔ edwam! Let's go shopping!

In Chapter 4 you learned numbers, time, days, and questions. Now it is time to use all of that in the place where Ghanaian life happens every day — the market.

Whether you are at Makola, a chop bar, or buying pure water from a street vendor, these are the phrases you will hear and need:

  • Wei yɛ sɛn? (How much is this?)
  • Ɛyɛ dɔ! Te boɔ no so kakra. (It is expensive! Reduce the price a little.)
  • Metua cedi aduonu. (I will pay 20 cedis.)
  • Medaase paa! (Thank you very much!)

What You Will Achieve

  • Name 30+ market items and food in Twi
  • Ask and understand prices in Ghana cedis and pesewas
  • Bargain politely and effectively
  • Handle transactions at markets, chop bars, and street vendors
  • Use the three key shopping verbs: tɔ (buy), tɔn (sell), tua (pay)

Kenkan · Abena Kɔ Makola (Abena Goes to Makola)

Read first — notice every price, every verb, every bargain

This passage uses every shopping skill in this chapter. Read it carefully before answering the comprehension questions below.

Makola Market, Accra — where every Twi phrase in this chapter comes alive

Ɛnnɛ yɛ Memeneda. Abena sɔre ntɛm efirisɛ ɔpɛ sɛ ɔkɔ Makola edwam. Today is Saturday. Abena wakes up early because she wants to go to Makola Market. Ɔhyɛ ne ntadeɛ, ɔdi ne anɔpa aduane kakra, na ɔkɔ. She dresses, eats a little breakfast, and goes. Ɔduru edwam no so, ɔhyia ɔdwadini baa baako. Ɔma no akwaaba: "Maakye! Wo ho te sɛn?" She arrives at the market and meets a female trader. She greets her: "Good morning! How are you?" Ɔdwadini no buae sɛ: "Maakye! Me ho yɛ. Dɛn na wopɛ?" The trader replied: "Good morning! I am fine. What are you looking for?" Abena kaa sɛ: "Mepɛ ntoosi ne gyene ne ngo kakra." Abena said: "I want tomatoes (ntoosi), onions, and a little palm oil." Ɔdwadini no kyerɛɛ no tomato fɛfɛ na ɔkaa sɛ: "Ntoosi no, ɛyɛ cedi aduonu." The trader showed her beautiful tomatoes (ntoosi) and said: "The tomatoes are 20 cedis." Abena tiemm sɛ: "Aaa! Ɛyɛ dɔ! Te boɔ no so kakra. Mepɛ sɛ metua cedi du nnum." Abena exclaimed (tiemm): "Aaa! That is expensive! Reduce the price a little. I want to pay 15 cedis." Ɔdwadini no dewenee kakra na ɔkaa sɛ: "Yoo, metua cedi du nsia." The trader thought (dewenee) for a moment and said: "OK, pay 16 cedis." Wɔte aseɛ na Abena tua cedi du nsia ma no. They agreed (Wɔte aseɛ) and Abena paid her 16 cedis. Wɔtɔɔ gyene ne ngo nso. Ne nyinaa, Abena tua cedi aduonu nsia. They also bought onions and palm oil. In total, Abena paid 26 cedis. Abena kaa sɛ: "Medaase paa!" Ɔdwadini no buae sɛ: "Yɛnda ase! Bra bio!" Abena said: "Thank you very much!" The trader replied: "It is my pleasure! Come again!" Abena firi edwam no na ɔkɔ chop bar. Ɔtɔɔ fufu ne nkwan ma ne ho. Ɛyɛ dɛ pa ara. Abena left the market and went to a chop bar. She bought fufu and soup for herself. It was very delicious.

Nsɛmbisa — Comprehension Questions

Different question types below — fill in the blank, short answer, calculate, and think-beyond. Answers are hidden until you click.

✏️ Fill in the blank

Ɛnnɛ yɛ  , na Abena kɔɔ   edwam. Ɔpɛɛ  ,  , ne  .

Answer
Ɛnnɛ yɛ Memeneda, na Abena kɔɔ Makola edwam. Ɔpɛɛ ntoosi, gyene, ne ngo.

💬 Short Answer

Twi: Ɔdwadini no kaa sɛ ntoosi no yɛ sɛn? Na Abena tiemm sɛ dɛn?

English: What price did the trader give for the tomatoes? What did Abena exclaim?

Answer
Ɔdwadini no kaa sɛ ntoosi no yɛ cedi aduonu (20 cedis).
Abena tiemm sɛ: "Ɛyɛ dɔ!" — meaning "It is expensive!"

🔄 Question & Answer

The bargaining went through three price stages. Put them in the correct order:

cedi du nnum (15) cedi aduonu (20) cedi du nsia (16)

Trader first price → Abena's offer → final agreed price

Answer
1️⃣ cedi aduonu (20) — trader's opening price
2️⃣ cedi du nnum (15) — Abena's counter-offer
3️⃣ cedi du nsia (16) — agreed final price
The trader came down but not all the way — a typical real bargain.

🧮 Calculate

Abena tua cedi du nsia ma ntoosi no. Ne nyinaa, ɔtua cedi aduonu nsia.
Abena paid 16 cedis for the tomatoes. She paid 26 cedis in total. How much did the onions and palm oil cost together? Write your working in Twi.

Answer
Cedi aduonu nsia tia cedi du nsia = cedi du (10 cedis)
26 − 16 = 10
Gyene ne ngo boɔ yɛɛ cedi du.

✏️ Fill in the blank

Wɔte aseɛ na Abena firi   no. Ɔkɔɔ  . Ɔtɔɔ   ne  . Ɛyɛ   pa ara.

Answer
Wɔte aseɛ na Abena firi edwam no. Ɔkɔɔ chop bar. Ɔtɔɔ fufu ne nkwan. Ɛyɛ dɛ pa ara.

✅ True or False?

Write T (true) or F (false) next to each. Answers are in the passage.

  1. Abena kɔɔ edwam wɔ Yawoada. ___
  2. Ɔdwadini no yɛɛ baa. ___
  3. Abena ne ɔdwadini no tenaa aseɛ wɔ cedi aduonu. ___
  4. Abena tɔɔ fufu ne nkwan wɔ chop bar. ___
  5. Ɔdwadini no kaa "Bra bio!" wɔ awieeɛ. ___
Answers
1. F — Ɛyɛ Memeneda, na Yawoada.  |  2. T — she was a female trader (ɔdwadini baa).  |  3. F — they agreed at cedi du nsia (16), not aduonu (20).  |  4. T.  |  5. T.

🧠 Think Beyond

Abena wanted to pay 15 cedis but paid 16 cedis. The trader wanted 20 but accepted 16. Who do you think got the better deal? Write 2–3 sentences explaining your answer in English or Twi.

Discussion points
There is no single right answer — this is intentional! Some points to consider:
• Abena came closest to her target (she was 1 cedi away from 15; the trader was 4 cedis away from 20).
• The trader kept the sale going and earned a loyal customer ("Bra bio!") — worth more than 4 cedis in the long run.
• Good bargaining is not about winning — it is about both sides feeling satisfied. Wɔte aseɛ — they agreed.

How to Say Prices

Pattern: Ɛyɛ cedi [number] — It is [number] cedis

Ɛyɛ cedi ɛnum. — It is 5 cedis.
Ɛyɛ cedi aduonu. — It is 20 cedis.
Ɛyɛ cedi aduonum. — It is 50 cedis.
Ɛyɛ cedi ɔha. — It is 100 cedis.
Ɛyɛ cedi aduonu ne pesewa aduonum. — It is 20 cedis 50 pesewas.
Ɛyɛ cedi ɛnum ne pesewa aduonu num. — It is 5 cedis 75 pesewas.

Nsɛmfua a Ɛho Hia — Key Shopping Phrases

Greet first, always — then ask

Always Greet Before Asking a Price

In a Ghanaian market, you MUST greet before asking anything. Skipping the greeting is rude.

Maakye! — Good morning (before noon)
Maaha! — Good afternoon
Maadwo! — Good evening
Wo ho te sɛn? — How are you?

Grammar Corner — The Three Shopping Verbs

tɔ (buy), tɔn (sell), tua (pay) — master these three

The Verb "tɔ" — To Buy

Follows the same pronoun-prefix pattern you learned in Chapter 3:

Pronoun + tɔContractedMeaning
me + tɔmetɔI buy
wo + tɔwotɔyou buy
ɔ + tɔɔtɔhe / she buys
yɛn + tɔyɛtɔwe buy
mo + tɔmotɔyou (plural) buy
wɔn + tɔwɔntɔthey buy

The Verb "tɔn" — To Sell

Pronoun + tɔnContractedMeaning
me + tɔnmetɔnI sell
wo + tɔnwotɔnyou sell
ɔ + tɔnɔtɔnhe / she sells
yɛn + tɔnyɛtɔnwe sell
mo + tɔnmotɔnyou (plural) sell
wɔn + tɔnwɔntɔnthey sell

The Verb "tua" — To Pay

Pronoun + tuaContractedMeaning
me + tuametuaI pay
wo + tuawotuayou pay
ɔ + tuaɔtuahe / she pays
yɛn + tuayɛtuawe pay
mo + tuamotuayou (plural) pay
wɔn + tuawɔntuathey pay

Imperatives — Polite Commands

At the market you constantly give and receive polite commands. These are imperative forms — the verb used directly without a pronoun subject:

Ma me nsuo. — Give me water.
Fa sika no. — Take the money.
Kyerɛ me wei. — Show me this.
Te boɔ no so. — Reduce the price.
Bra bio. — Come again.
Nante yie. — Walk well.

Negative Shopping Sentences

Add the n- prefix (from Chapter 3) to shopping verbs:

PositiveNegative
Metɔ. (I buy.)Mentɔ. (I do not buy.)
Metua. (I pay.)Mentua. (I do not pay.)
Mepɛ wei. (I want this.)Menpɛ wei. (I do not want this.)
Metɔ tomato nnɛ. (I buy tomatoes today.)Mentɔ tomato nnɛ. (I am not buying tomatoes today.)

Word Order in Shopping Sentences

Pattern: Subject + Verb + Object + Quantity / Price

Me + tɔ + tomato + kakra → Metɔ tomato kakra.
Me + pɛ + aburoo + mmienu → Mepɛ aburoo mmienu.
Ɛ + yɛ + sika + aduonu → Ɛyɛ cedi aduonu.
