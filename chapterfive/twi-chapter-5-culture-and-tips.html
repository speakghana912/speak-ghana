Cultural Notes

🙏 Always Greet First

  • Never walk up to a stall and ask a price without greeting. This is considered very rude.
  • Always start with Maakye / Maaha / Maadwo depending on the time of day.
  • Then ask about the person: Wo ho te sɛn? or Ɛte sɛn?
  • Only then begin your shopping with Mepɛ... or Mehwehwɛ...

🤝 Bargaining is Normal

  • The first price is almost never the final price. Bargaining is expected and welcomed.
  • Start at around 60–70% of the asking price and meet somewhere in the middle.
  • Always stay friendly. A smile and Mepa wo kyɛw go a long way.
  • If you cannot agree, it is fine to say Yoo, medaase and walk away politely.
  • Never bargain aggressively — the goal is agreement, not winning.

✋ Use Your Right Hand

  • Always give and receive money with your right hand.
  • The left hand is considered unclean in Ghanaian culture.
  • If you must use both hands, support your right wrist with your left as a sign of respect.
  • This applies to giving food, gifts, and greeting elders too — not just money.

🏪 Famous Markets in Ghana

  • Makola Market (Accra): The biggest market in Accra — food, cloth, household items, electronics. Very busy.
  • Kaneshie Market: Great for fresh produce and second-hand clothing. Less crowded than Makola.
  • Agbogbloshie: Known as the Onion Market — huge piles of onions, yams, and produce.
  • Osu Night Market: Open in the evenings — street food, small items, lively atmosphere.
  • Kumasi Central Market: One of the largest in West Africa — everything from kente to fresh food.

🍲 Chop Bar Culture

  • A chop bar is a local restaurant serving traditional Ghanaian food — simple, affordable, and delicious.
  • You order at a counter or window, pay, and collect your food in a bowl or on a plate.
  • Many people eat with their right hand only. A washing basin is usually provided.
  • Saying Adane no yɛ dɛ! (The food is delicious!) is always appreciated by the cook.

💧 Street Vendors and Pure Water

  • Pure water is sold in small plastic sachets everywhere — you bite a corner and drink.
  • Street vendors call out their items as they walk: "Nsuo! Nsuo!"
  • Airtime sellers are common at bus stops and markets — just say the network and amount.
  • Carry small notes (cedi 5, 10, 20) — vendors often do not have change for large bills.
  • Mobile money (MoMo) is widely accepted — "MoMo yɛ?" means "Can I pay with mobile money?"

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Mistake: Forgetting to greet before asking the price. ✅ Fix: Always start with Maakye! or Maaha! then Wo ho te sɛn? — only then ask Wei yɛ sɛn?
❌ Mistake: Saying Te boɔ ase instead of Te boɔ no so. ✅ Fix: The correct phrase to reduce the price is Te boɔ no so kakra. To say the price is high, use Boɔ no wɔ soro.
❌ Mistake: Confusing tɔ (buy) and tɔn (sell). ✅ Fix: Metɔ = I buy. Ɔtɔn = He/She sells. The extra "n" makes all the difference.
❌ Mistake: Using double-n in negative forms — menntɔ / menntua. ✅ Fix: Single n- prefix only: mentɔ (I don't buy), mentua (I don't pay), menpɛ (I don't want).
❌ Mistake: Saying Yɛnda ase meaning "we thank you" — it actually means "it is my pleasure." ✅ Fix: When a seller says Yɛnda aseɛ after you pay, it means "it is my pleasure" — not "we thank you." It is a warm closing, like "you are welcome."
❌ Mistake: Not knowing your numbers well enough to handle prices quickly. ✅ Fix: Practice numbers 1–100 before going to the market. Sellers talk fast and prices come quickly — you need the numbers to be automatic.
❌ Mistake: Forgetting to say thank you after buying. ✅ Fix: Always end with Medaase or Medaase paa! — it is expected and shows respect.
C

Tips for Success

🛒

Go to a real market and use your Twi

Go to a Ghanaian market, chop bar, or street vendor and try one complete transaction in Twi. Greet, ask the price, bargain if you can, pay, say thank you. Even one sentence will earn you goodwill — and probably a better price.

📝

Write your shopping list in Twi first

Before you go to the market, write your list in Twi: Mepɛ ntoosi, gyene, ne nsuo. Knowing what you want to say before you arrive makes the real conversation much less stressful.

🔢

Drill prices every day

Pick 5 random numbers between 1 and 100 each morning and say them as cedi amounts in Twi: Ɛyɛ cedi aduasa nwɔtwe. Do this for a week and prices will feel automatic.

😊

Smile while bargaining

Bargaining in Ghana is friendly, not a fight. A smile and a warm tone will get you further than demanding a lower price. If a seller laughs with you, that is a good sign — you are building a relationship, not just buying something.

💬

Learn the credit phrase

In Ghana, it is common to ask for things on credit from people you know well. Fa firi me (give me on credit) is useful but use it carefully — only with sellers you have a relationship with, never on a first visit.

🎯

Master Wowɔ...?

Wowɔ ntoosi? (Do you have tomatoes?) is one of the most useful market phrases. Practice asking this for 10 different items until it feels natural — it works in any shopping situation.
