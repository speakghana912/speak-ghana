👥 Twi Noun Plurals Part II: Advanced Person Nouns

🎯 Advanced plural formations for person-related nouns (Mo-/N- prefix, -foɔ suffix, -nom suffix, compound nouns)

📚 Introduction

This second part covers more complex plural formations for person-related nouns, involving specific prefix changes or suffixes that denote a collective group.

🔤 Rule 1: Nouns Using the "Mo-" or "N-" Prefix

Some nouns describing types of people or relationships use a nasal prefix change, particularly those that don't fit the standard ɔ- to a- pattern.

👨‍🏫 Rule 2: Professional & Group Suffixes (-foɔ)

Some nouns already end in -foɔ in the singular and remain the same or change the prefix to indicate plurality.

Singular (Biako)Plural (Bebree)English Translation
ɔkuafoɔakuafoɔfarmer / farmers
ɔhianiahiafoɔpoor person / the poor
ɔdeefoɔadeefoɔgenerous person / people

📌 Note: Notice that the singular prefix ɔ- changes to a- in the plural.
👨‍👩‍👧 Rule 3: The "-nom" Suffix for Relatives

For many family and kinship terms, the plural is formed by adding the suffix -nom. This is one of the most consistent rules for familial relations.

🔗 Rule 4: Pluralizing Compound Person Nouns

When a noun is made of two words (a compound noun), usually only the first part or the primary noun in the phrase undergoes the plural change.

Singular (Biako)Plural (Bebree)English Translation
ɔkyerɛkyerɛni-paninakyerɛkyerɛfoɔ-mpanyinHeadteacher(s)
ɔsraani-panyinasraafoɔ-mpanyinOfficer(s) / Senior soldier(s)
ɔkyerɛkyerɛni-panin (Headteacher)
Singular: ɔkyerɛkyerɛni-panin → Plural: akyerɛkyerɛfoɔ-mpanyin
📌 Pattern: The first part changes from singular to plural form, while the second part (panin → mpanyin) also changes accordingly.
